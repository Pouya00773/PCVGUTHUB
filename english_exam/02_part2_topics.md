## A) EU-Themen

### 1. Describe the EU triangle (Parliament, Commission, Council)

> The EU triangle describes the three most important institutions of the European Union
> and how they make laws together.
>
> First, the **European Commission** is like the government of the EU. It **proposes new laws**
> and makes sure that member states follow the rules. Each member state sends one commissioner.
>
> Second, the **European Parliament** represents the **citizens** of the EU. The people elect
> it directly every five years. It discusses and votes on the laws.
>
> Third, the **Council of the European Union** represents the **governments of the member
> states**. Ministers from all member states meet there and also vote on the laws.
>
> To sum up: the Commission proposes a law, and the Parliament and the Council must both
> agree before it becomes a law. This is why we call it a triangle.

**Vokabeln:** institution = Institution · to propose a law = ein Gesetz vorschlagen ·
citizen = Bürger · to elect = wählen · member state = Mitgliedsstaat · to represent = vertreten

**Merkhilfe:** Commission = **schlägt vor** · Parliament = **Bürger** · Council = **Regierungen**

### 2. Describe the main historic steps from the ECSC to the present EU

> The European Union developed step by step over more than 70 years.
>
> It started in **1951** with the **European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC)**. Six countries –
> Germany, France, Italy and the Benelux countries – put their coal and steel production
> together, so that war between them would become impossible.
>
> In **1957**, the same countries signed the **Treaty of Rome** and created the **European
> Economic Community (EEC)** – a common market with free trade.
>
> In **1993**, the **Maastricht Treaty** created the **European Union** as we know it today,
> with cooperation not only in the economy but also in politics.
>
> In **1999/2002**, the **Euro** was introduced, first as electronic money, then as cash.
> Over the years, more and more countries joined – today the EU has **27 member states**.
> In **2020**, the United Kingdom left the EU – this was called **Brexit**.
>
> To sum up: the EU grew from a small economic community of six countries into a political
> and economic union of 27 countries.

**Vokabeln:** coal and steel = Kohle und Stahl · treaty = Vertrag · common market = gemeinsamer Markt ·
to join = beitreten · to leave = austreten · to develop = sich entwickeln

**Merkhilfe (Jahreszahlen):** 1951 ECSC → 1957 Rom (EEC) → 1993 Maastricht (EU) → 2002 Euro-Bargeld → 2020 Brexit

### 3. What has the EU achieved since the foundation of the ECSC in the 1950s?

> The EU has achieved a lot since the 1950s.
>
> The most important achievement is **peace**: the member states have not fought a war
> against each other for over 70 years. The EU even won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2012.
>
> Second, the **single market**: goods, services, capital and people can move **freely**
> between member states. These are called the **four freedoms**.
>
> Third, many countries share a **common currency**, the Euro, which makes trade and
> travelling easier.
>
> Also, EU citizens can **live, work and study in any member state**. Programmes like
> **Erasmus** allow students to study abroad. And thanks to the **Schengen Agreement**,
> there are no border controls between most member states.
>
> To sum up: the EU has brought peace, economic growth and personal freedom to its citizens.

**Vokabeln:** achievement = Errungenschaft · peace = Frieden · single market = Binnenmarkt ·
four freedoms = vier Grundfreiheiten · common currency = gemeinsame Währung · border control = Grenzkontrolle

### 4. What role does the Euro play in the world?

> The Euro is one of the most important currencies in the world.
>
> It is the official currency of **20 EU countries**, the so-called **Eurozone**, and about
> 350 million people use it every day.
>
> After the US Dollar, the Euro is the **second most important currency** in the world.
> Many countries hold Euros as a **reserve currency**, and a large part of international
> trade is paid in Euros.
>
> The Euro makes life easier: companies can trade **without exchanging money**, prices are
> easy to compare between countries, and tourists do not have to change money.
>
> The **European Central Bank (ECB)** in Frankfurt controls the Euro and tries to keep
> **inflation** low and prices stable.
>
> To sum up: the Euro is a symbol of European integration and a global currency, second
> only to the US Dollar.

**Vokabeln:** currency = Währung · reserve currency = Reservewährung · exchange rate = Wechselkurs ·
to compare prices = Preise vergleichen · stable = stabil · European Central Bank = Europäische Zentralbank

### 5. What were the causes and effects of Brexit?

> Brexit means the exit of Britain from the European Union. The British people voted for it
> in a **referendum** in 2016, and the UK finally left the EU in **2020**.
>
> There were several **causes**: Many British people wanted **more national control** over
> their own laws and their borders. **Immigration** was a big topic – some people were
> afraid of too many immigrants. Others did not want to pay so much money to the EU.
> The slogan of the Brexit campaign was "Take back control".
>
> The **effects** were mostly negative for the UK: **Trade** with the EU became more
> difficult and expensive because of new **customs controls** and paperwork. Many companies
> moved to the EU. There were problems with **workers** because many EU citizens left the
> country. Also, British students lost easy access to the Erasmus programme.
>
> To sum up: Brexit gave the UK more independence on paper, but economically it created
> many problems for both sides – especially for the UK.

**Vokabeln:** referendum = Volksabstimmung · cause = Ursache · effect = Auswirkung ·
immigration = Einwanderung · customs = Zoll · independence = Unabhängigkeit

## B) Marketing-Themen

### 6. Use the marketing mix to market a product of your choice

> The marketing mix consists of the **four Ps**: **Product, Price, Place and Promotion**.
> I would like to explain it with an example: a new **energy drink**.
>
> **Product:** The product itself. Our energy drink is sugar-free, has natural ingredients
> and a modern design. This makes it different from other drinks.
>
> **Price:** We use a medium price, similar to Red Bull, because a very low price would look
> like bad quality. At the start we could offer special discounts.
>
> **Place:** Where customers can buy it. We sell it in supermarkets, at petrol stations,
> in gyms and online.
>
> **Promotion:** How we tell people about it. We advertise on **social media** with
> influencers, because our target group is young people. We also sponsor sport events
> and give away free samples at universities.
>
> To sum up: only if all four Ps fit together, the product can be successful on the market.

**Vokabeln:** marketing mix = Marketing-Mix · promotion = Werbung/Verkaufsförderung ·
target group = Zielgruppe · discount = Rabatt · free sample = Gratisprobe · ingredient = Zutat

**Merkhilfe:** 4 Ps = **P**rodukt, **P**reis, **P**latz (Vertrieb), **P**romotion (Werbung)

### 7. How can companies divide their consumer markets into segments and what advantages do they gain?

> Market segmentation means dividing a big market into smaller groups of customers with
> similar needs.
>
> There are four main ways to segment a market: First, **demographic** segmentation – by age,
> gender, income or family status. Second, **geographic** segmentation – by country, region
> or city size. Third, **psychographic** segmentation – by lifestyle, values and interests.
> And fourth, **behavioural** segmentation – by buying behaviour, for example how often
> people buy a product.
>
> The advantages are clear: Companies can develop products that **fit the needs** of each
> group better. Advertising becomes **more effective and cheaper**, because you only talk to
> people who are really interested. And the company can find **niches** where there is
> little competition.
>
> To sum up: segmentation helps companies to use their money more efficiently and to make
> their customers happier.

**Vokabeln:** segmentation = Segmentierung · needs = Bedürfnisse · income = Einkommen ·
behaviour = Verhalten · niche = Nische · competition = Konkurrenz/Wettbewerb

### 8. Which different ways of doing market research do you know and how effective are they?

> Market research means collecting information about customers and markets. There are two
> main types: **primary** and **secondary** research.
>
> **Primary research** means collecting **new data** yourself, for example with **surveys,
> interviews, focus groups or product tests**. It is very effective because the data is
> fresh and exactly about your question – but it is **expensive and takes time**.
>
> **Secondary research** means using data that **already exists**, for example statistics,
> studies, newspaper articles or data from the internet. It is **fast and cheap**, but the
> data may be old or not exactly what you need.
>
> How effective they are depends on the situation: for a quick overview, secondary research
> is enough. For an important decision, like launching a new product, companies should also
> do primary research.
>
> To sum up: the best way is usually a combination of both – start with secondary research
> and then fill the gaps with primary research.

**Vokabeln:** market research = Marktforschung · survey = Umfrage · focus group = Fokusgruppe ·
data = Daten · to launch a product = ein Produkt einführen · combination = Kombination

### 9. How and where can a survey be conducted?

> A survey is a list of questions that a company asks people to learn about their opinions
> and wishes.
>
> **How:** A survey can be done **face-to-face**, for example on the street or in a shop.
> It can be done **by telephone**, **by mail** with a paper questionnaire, or – most common
> today – **online**, for example by e-mail, on a website or on social media.
>
> **Where:** Good places are shopping centres, city centres, universities, trade fairs, or
> directly at the point of sale. Online surveys can reach people everywhere.
>
> Each method has pros and cons: face-to-face gives detailed answers but is expensive.
> Online surveys are cheap and fast, but many people ignore them, and you cannot always
> control who answers.
>
> To sum up: the best method depends on the target group and the budget – young people are
> easy to reach online, older people maybe better by phone or face-to-face.

**Vokabeln:** to conduct a survey = eine Umfrage durchführen · questionnaire = Fragebogen ·
face-to-face = persönlich · trade fair = Messe · point of sale = Verkaufsort · budget = Budget

### 10. How and where can a product be advertised?

> There are many ways to advertise a product, and companies usually combine several of them.
>
> **Classic media:** television, radio, newspapers and magazines. TV reaches many people,
> but it is very expensive.
>
> **Outdoor advertising:** posters and billboards on the street, in bus stops or on public
> transport.
>
> **Online advertising:** this is the most important channel today – ads on social media
> like Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, cooperation with **influencers**, search engine ads
> on Google, and e-mail newsletters. Online ads can target exactly the right group and you
> can measure the success.
>
> **Other ways:** sponsoring of sport or music events, free samples, and special offers in
> the shop.
>
> To sum up: where you advertise depends on the target group – for young people social
> media works best, for older people TV and newspapers can still be effective.

**Vokabeln:** to advertise = werben · advertisement (ad) = Werbeanzeige · billboard = Werbetafel ·
channel = Kanal · to measure = messen · special offer = Sonderangebot

## C) Wirtschafts-Themen

### 11. What are the most important economic indicators?

> Economic indicators are numbers that show how healthy an economy is.
>
> The most important one is the **GDP – gross domestic product**. It measures the total
> value of all goods and services produced in a country in one year. If GDP grows, the
> economy is doing well.
>
> The second is the **unemployment rate** – the percentage of people who want to work but
> cannot find a job.
>
> The third is the **inflation rate** – it shows how fast prices are rising. High inflation
> means people can buy less for their money.
>
> Other important indicators are the **balance of trade** (exports minus imports), the
> **interest rate** set by the central bank, and **consumer confidence** – how positive
> people feel about the future.
>
> To sum up: governments, companies and investors watch these indicators to make good
> decisions.

**Vokabeln:** economic indicator = Wirtschaftsindikator · GDP (gross domestic product) = BIP (Bruttoinlandsprodukt) ·
unemployment rate = Arbeitslosenquote · inflation = Inflation · interest rate = Zinssatz · balance of trade = Handelsbilanz

### 12. What is a business cycle?

> A business cycle describes the ups and downs of an economy over time. It has **four phases**.
>
> First, the **expansion** or boom phase (recovery/upswing): the economy grows, companies produce more,
> people find jobs and spend money.
>
> Second, the **peak**: the economy is at its highest point. Prices often rise and there
> can be inflation.
>
> Third, the **recession** or downturn: the economy shrinks, companies sell less, people
> lose their jobs and spend less money.
>
> Fourth, the **trough** – the lowest point. After that, the economy recovers and a new
> cycle begins.
>
> A cycle can take several years, and the state tries to make the cycle smoother – for
> example, the central bank lowers interest rates in a recession so that people and
> companies spend more money again.
>
> To sum up: the business cycle shows that growth and crisis follow each other regularly
> in a market economy.

**Vokabeln:** business cycle = Konjunkturzyklus · expansion/upswing = Aufschwung · peak = Hochpunkt ·
recession/downturn = Abschwung/Rezession · trough = Tiefpunkt · to recover = sich erholen

**Merkhilfe:** Aufschwung → Hochpunkt → Abschwung → Tiefpunkt → wieder von vorn

### 13. What is a recession? What industries are recession-proof and why?

> A recession is a phase in which the economy **shrinks**. Economists speak of a recession
> when the GDP falls for **two quarters in a row**. In a recession, companies sell less,
> unemployment rises, and people spend less money.
>
> But some industries are **recession-proof** – this means they stay stable even in bad
> times. Why? Because they sell things that people **always need**, no matter how much
> money they have.
>
> Examples are: the **food industry** – people always have to eat. **Healthcare and
> pharmaceutical companies** – people get sick in good times and in bad times. **Energy
> and water** – everybody needs electricity and heating. Also **discount supermarkets**
> often profit, because people look for cheaper alternatives.
>
> To sum up: industries that satisfy basic needs are recession-proof, while industries that
> sell luxury goods, cars or holidays suffer most in a recession.

**Vokabeln:** to shrink = schrumpfen · two quarters in a row = zwei Quartale in Folge ·
recession-proof = krisensicher · basic needs = Grundbedürfnisse · healthcare = Gesundheitswesen · luxury goods = Luxusgüter

### 14. Who suffers most in a recession? Who benefits, and why?

> In a recession there are losers, but surprisingly also some winners.
>
> **Who suffers most?** First, **workers** who lose their jobs – especially people with
> low qualifications and young people who look for their first job. Second, companies that
> sell **luxury goods**, cars, furniture or holidays, because people stop buying expensive
> things. Third, **small businesses** and restaurants, because they have small financial
> reserves. Also, the **state** suffers: it gets less taxes but has to pay more social
> benefits.
>
> **Who benefits?** **Discount stores** and cheap brands, because customers want to save
> money. **Repair services**, because people repair old things instead of buying new ones.
> Companies with a lot of cash can **buy other companies cheaply**. And people with a safe
> job can profit from falling prices.
>
> To sum up: a recession hits the weakest people hardest, while companies that offer cheap
> solutions can even grow.

**Vokabeln:** to suffer = leiden · to benefit = profitieren · social benefits = Sozialleistungen ·
taxes = Steuern · reserves = Rücklagen · brand = Marke

### 15. The demographic change: describe the main trends

> Demographic change describes how the population of a country changes over time. In
> Germany and Europe, there are three main trends.
>
> First, **people live longer** than in the past because of better healthcare and living
> conditions. Life expectancy is now over 80 years.
>
> Second, the **birth rate is low** – families have fewer children than in the past.
>
> The result is an **ageing society**: there are more and more old people and fewer young
> people. This is a problem for the **pension system**, because fewer workers must pay for
> more pensioners. It also creates a **shortage of skilled workers**.
>
> The third trend is **migration**: people from other countries move to Germany. This makes
> the society more diverse and can help against the lack of workers.
>
> To sum up: our society is getting older and more diverse, and politics and companies must
> react – for example with a higher retirement age, immigration of skilled workers, and
> family support.

**Vokabeln:** demographic change = demografischer Wandel · life expectancy = Lebenserwartung ·
birth rate = Geburtenrate · ageing society = alternde Gesellschaft · pension = Rente ·
shortage of skilled workers = Fachkräftemangel

### 16. Describe the role of the underground economy

> The underground economy – also called shadow economy or black economy – includes all
> economic activities that are **not reported to the state**.
>
> There are two parts: First, **illegal activities** like drug dealing or smuggling. Second –
> and much bigger – **legal work that is not declared**, for example when a painter renovates
> a flat and is paid **cash in hand**, without taxes.
>
> Why does it exist? People want to **avoid taxes** and social security payments, and some
> people cannot work legally.
>
> The **effects** are mostly negative: the state loses **billions in taxes**, which are
> missing for schools, hospitals and roads. Honest companies have a **competitive
> disadvantage** because black workers are cheaper. And the workers themselves have **no
> insurance and no pension**.
>
> To sum up: the underground economy is large in every country – in Germany it is about
> ten percent of GDP – and it damages the state, honest companies and often the workers
> themselves.

**Vokabeln:** underground/shadow economy = Schattenwirtschaft · undeclared work = Schwarzarbeit ·
cash in hand = Barzahlung ohne Rechnung · to avoid taxes = Steuern vermeiden · insurance = Versicherung ·
competitive disadvantage = Wettbewerbsnachteil

## D) Internationaler Handel

### 17. What aspects should be considered if a company wants to break into foreign markets?

> Breaking into a foreign market is a big chance, but a company should check many aspects
> before.
>
> First, **market research**: Is there demand for the product? How strong is the local
> **competition**? How much can customers pay?
>
> Second, **culture and language**: The company must adapt its product, its advertising and
> its business behaviour to the local culture. A famous mistake is a brand name that has a
> bad meaning in the local language.
>
> Third, **laws and politics**: Every country has different laws, taxes, **customs duties**
> and product standards. The political situation should be stable.
>
> Fourth, the **market entry strategy**: The company can simply **export**, work with a
> local **partner or licensee**, open a **joint venture**, or build its own **subsidiary**.
> Each option has different costs and risks.
>
> To sum up: good preparation is everything – companies that do their homework on culture,
> law and competition have much better chances abroad.

**Vokabeln:** to break into a market = in einen Markt eintreten · demand = Nachfrage ·
to adapt = anpassen · customs duty = Zoll(gebühr) · joint venture = Gemeinschaftsunternehmen ·
subsidiary = Tochtergesellschaft

### 18. How does international trade differ from domestic trade?

> Domestic trade means buying and selling **inside one country**. International trade means
> trade **across borders** – and this makes many things more complicated.
>
> First, **currency**: in international trade you often pay in a foreign currency, so there
> is an **exchange rate risk** – the value of money can change.
>
> Second, **laws and customs**: every country has its own laws, product standards, import
> rules and **customs duties**. In domestic trade there is only one legal system.
>
> Third, **language and culture**: contracts, negotiations and marketing must work in a
> foreign language and culture.
>
> Fourth, **transport and payment**: distances are longer, transport is more expensive and
> takes more time, and payment is less safe because it is harder to check a foreign partner.
>
> To sum up: international trade offers bigger markets and more customers, but it also
> brings more risks, more costs and more bureaucracy than domestic trade.

**Vokabeln:** domestic trade = Binnenhandel · across borders = grenzüberschreitend ·
exchange rate = Wechselkurs · negotiation = Verhandlung · legal system = Rechtssystem

### 19. What risks should exporters and importers take into account before signing a trade deal?

> Before signing an international trade deal, exporters and importers should think about
> several risks.
>
> First, the **payment risk** (credit risk): the foreign customer may pay late or not at all,
> and it is difficult to go to court in another country. A solution is a **letter of credit**
> from a bank or payment in advance.
>
> Second, the **exchange rate risk**: between signing the contract and payment, the value
> of the currency can change, so you get less money than planned.
>
> Third, the **transport risk**: goods can be damaged, lost or delayed on the long way.
> Good insurance and clear delivery terms (**Incoterms**) help here.
>
> Fourth, the **political risk**: wars, sanctions, new customs duties or import bans can
> stop the deal. Also, the legal systems and product standards can be different.
>
> To sum up: international deals can be very profitable, but exporters and importers should
> reduce these risks with insurance, secure payment methods and good contracts.

**Vokabeln:** trade deal = Handelsgeschäft · payment risk = Zahlungsrisiko · letter of credit = Akkreditiv ·
payment in advance = Vorkasse · to delay = verzögern · sanction = Sanktion · contract = Vertrag
